# Crisp Findings

Terse index of what we know, what we're answering, what's next. Each finding is one statement + one or two lines of proof + intuition. Full detail per finding lives in its appendix below; original full reasoning in [claude-notes.md](claude-notes.md).

## Findings

**F1 — Weights alone encode redundancy.** A BiLSTM reading only weight matrices prunes \~60–79% of an MNIST MLP at\<0.1–3.7pp drop. No data, no gradients needed.

**F2 — pg19's zero-shot CE screening numbers (50K-token sample) were unrepresentative; the real gap survives at proper sample size.** `zero_shot_ce_check.py`'s original 50K-token check gave GPT-2 CE=3.326 (ppl 27.83), OPT-125M CE=2.910 (ppl 18.35). Re-run at 245K tokens (matching the sweep scripts' own `n_test_tokens` default): GPT-2 CE=3.9255 (ppl 50.68), OPT-125M CE=3.4508 (ppl 31.53) — both roughly doubled, confirming pg19's book-to-book heterogeneity makes a small deterministic-streamed sample unrepresentative, not a GPT-2-specific bug. The B6-relevant part survives: gap = GPT-2_CE − OPT_CE was +0.416 nats at 50K, +0.4746 nats at 245K — same direction, similar (slightly larger) magnitude. Treat 27.83/18.35 as superseded; 50.68/31.53 are the correct zero-shot pg19 baselines to compare sweep results against.

**F3 — Pruner-capacity ablation (B7b) doesn't cleanly resolve whether high-λ underperformance is a capacity bottleneck or an inherently harder landscape — it surfaces a third, unanticipated confound: convergence-window validity is itself capacity-dependent.** `train_pruner_pg19_capacity_scaling.py` (embed_dim=128, lstm_hidden=256 — 2.257× base params; the script's own default of 144/216 was overridden for this run) vs. base-size pruner, matched at 4 λ (0.05, 0.3, 0.8, 1.6) × 2 models. Mixed result: 3/8 points improved (gpt2 λ=0.8: ppl −5.52%, fewer steps; opt125m λ=0.05: −1.19%; opt125m λ=1.6: −3.43%), 3/8 got worse (gpt2 λ=0.05: +1.80%; gpt2 λ=1.6: +1.36%; opt125m λ=0.3: **+6.22%**), 2/8 flat. No clean split by "flagged high-λ trouble point" — GPT-2 and OPT-125M disagree on which of 0.8/1.6 benefits from more capacity. The concerning case: opt125m λ=0.3 (a "should stay stable" control point) converged in *fewer* steps (900→600) at *worse* ppl, and its gap-diagnostic trajectory shows why — pct_pruned climbed 28.6%→32.2%→35.5% in a steady monotonic trend, not noise, yet still satisfied the block-mean flatness check (5%/1pp tolerance, 250-step window) and got called converged mid-climb. Mechanism: a bigger/more expressive pruner can move through weight space faster per step, so a window size tuned against the base pruner's dynamics can mistake a genuinely-still-moving trend for a plateau simply because more of the trajectory completes before the window closes. Window size and pruner capacity were both held "fixed" as the wrong assumption — window validity isn't capacity-invariant. Next step (not yet run): rerun the 2x-capacity ablation with a longer/stricter window to see if that closes the opt125m λ=0.3 gap, before drawing further conclusions about H1.

**F4 — A third capacity point (half-size pruner, tmp5) shows NO meaningful, monotonic effect of pruner capacity on %pruned, ppl quality, or convergence speed — F3's apparent "2x helps at some λ" pattern looks like noise now that there's a third point to check it against.** Three-way comparison (half=0.4678x, base=1x, 2.26x — see F3 — all at the same 4λ × 2 models = 8 matched points, 1 seed each): 0/8 points show a monotonic half<base<2.26x ordering on %pruned (avg spread 8.6% of base, non-directional); 0/8 monotonic on ppl rise (avg spread only 4.2% of base — the cleanest null of the three); steps-to-converge is the noisiest metric by far (avg spread 32.8%) but *still* only 3/8 monotonic, i.e. the metric with the most absolute variation shows the least systematic relationship to capacity. A genuine capacity effect should move the same direction from half→base→2.26x; it doesn't, anywhere, on any of the three axes. Reframes H1/B7b: within this ~5x capacity range, pruner size is not the binding constraint on outcome — effort is better spent on F3's window-validity mechanism (a fixed 250-step convergence window may not mean the same thing at different pruner sizes / different λ difficulty) than on further capacity scaling. Caveat: single seed per config throughout (as with F3) — cannot cleanly separate "truly no effect" from "a small real effect below single-seed noise," but the noise floor itself (8.6%/4.2%) is now at least characterized.

**F5 — Layer-budget vs. within-layer-selection decomposition: WITHIN-layer neuron selection is doing critical, irreplaceable work — F4's capacity-insensitivity is NOT evidence the pruner "isn't doing anything."** Motivated directly by F4's suspicious-looking null result. Architecturally the pruner's two jobs are separable (BiLSTM's `context_bias` is one scalar per layer, cannot discriminate within a layer — 100% of within-layer selection is the row-encoder's job). Test: take a trained pruner checkpoint's *exact* per-layer budget (`per_layer_kept`, the BiLSTM's discovered counts) but replace WITHIN-layer neuron choice with simple activation-magnitude ranking (same criterion as F22's baseline — mean post-ReLU activation, keep highest/prune lowest — NOT weight magnitude), same per-layer count as the real mask. Checkpoints: `tmp3/pg19_converge_sweep/{gpt2,opt125m}/lambda_0.3/pruner.pt` (base-size pruner, mid-range λ). Result: catastrophic degradation for both models — gpt2: real pruned ppl 40.698 → decomposed 87.706 (**+47.0**, worse than not pruning at all, orig=50.683); opt125m: real 35.538 → decomposed 48.707 (**+13.2**, also worse than orig=31.528). Handing the heuristic the *correct* per-layer budget (an advantage F22's own baseline never had) still fails badly — bigger gap than F22 found even. Conclusion: within-layer selection is real, substantial, necessary work a magnitude-style heuristic cannot approximate, even with perfect budget information. F4's capacity result is better read as "this particular scoring function, whatever it is, happens to be representable by a very small network (down to embed_dim=32)" — not "the row-encoder isn't computing anything meaningful." Design notes in engineering_decisions.md.

**F6 — Llama-2-7B/WikiText-2, matched against DISP-LLM's own published numbers on the identical model+dataset, beats DISP-LLM at every converged operating point up to ~29% total-param pruned — and the one point past that which loses is independently diagnosable as broken, not a real data point.** 9-λ convergence-based sweep (`train_pruner_llama2_7b_wikitext2.py`, dense ppl=4.903), converted to total-param-pruned % via Llama-2-7B's 64.24% MLP parameter share, compared against DISP-LLM Table 1's LLaMA-2-7B/no-weight-update column (dense=5.12; 20%→6.10, 30%→6.85, 40%→8.11, 50%→9.84, linearly interpolated to our exact %s). Result: **8 of 8 converged points beat the interpolated DISP-LLM curve** — by −0.45 ppl at the low end (5.4-6.6% total pruned), narrowing steadily to a near-tie at 28.77% (+0.016 ppl, essentially noise). The 9th point (λ=1.4, 32.58% total) loses by +0.494 ppl, but **never converged** — `lr_state` stayed `pre_decay` for the full 18,000-step cap, meaning `check_converged` never returned `True` even once (not a false-trigger-then-fail like B9 was built to catch — a total absence of any trigger). Diagnosed the failure directly from `gap_diagnostic.csv`: `pct_pruned` in the last 20 checkpoints (steps 14200-18000) still swings an 8.2pp band (48.5-56.7%) with essentially the same noise level as the *first* 20 checkpoints (mean 51.4%→52.8%, stdev 2.81→1.74 — barely tightening after 14,000 more steps), and `max_layer_delta_pct` averages ~30pp *even in the final checkpoints* — individual layers are still swinging by up to 47.8pp between 200-step checkpoints at step 18,000. This is a genuinely oscillating equilibrium, not a slow monotonic drift more steps would resolve. Mechanism (not yet confirmed, best current hypothesis): at λ=1.4 the sparsity term (λ·mean(gate) ≈ 0.66-0.8) is now comparable in magnitude to the CE-cost term (≈0.37-0.46), and the LR is held at a constant `1e-3` regardless of λ (never scaled down for high-λ operating points, since the B9 decay mechanism never triggers to bring it down) — a plausible recipe for sustained oscillation around a ridge in the loss landscape rather than convergence to a fixed point. **Directly re-tested the "increase pruner capacity" fix against F3/F4's own prior evidence and it doesn't hold**: F3/F4 already found no meaningful monotonic capacity effect on %pruned/ppl/convergence-speed across an 8-point matched grid (half/base/2.26x) — capacity controls representational expressiveness of the row-encoder/BiLSTM, not the STE-threshold oscillation dynamics under a fixed-LR gradient signal, so there's no mechanistic reason to expect it to help here either. The indicated fix is LR/λ-coupling (lower or λ-scaled initial LR for high-sparsity operating points), not architecture size — an optimization-dynamics problem, not a capacity problem. Caveat: F3/F4 were run at GPT-2/OPT-125M/pg19 scale, not 7B — applying that finding here is a reasonable prior, not a proven transfer. Single seed throughout (as with every convergence-based sweep in this project) — no seed-variance estimate at this operating point. Full sweep, DISP-LLM comparison table, and mechanism note in `past-work.md`/`final-paper-direction.md`.

**F24 — Checked three external SOTA comparison targets (structured/unlearned, unstructured/unlearned, unstructured/learned) for a fair head-to-head against our 7B-scale results — none currently qualify, for three different reasons.** Pulled real numbers directly from primary sources (PDF text extraction / WebFetch, not abstracts) before attempting any comparison, per the "only compare if the experiment is the same conditions" rule:
- **GISP** (structured+unlearned, `docs/papers/gisp_wang2025.pdf` Tables 4–5) DOES test the right model (Llama2-7B, WikiText-2) — but reports dense baseline ppl **12.19**, vs. our own **4.903** and DISP-LLM's **5.12** on the same model/dataset — a ~2.5× unexplained gap. Searched the full paper text for the eval context-length/stride spec that would explain this; not stated anywhere in the main text or appendix (their own phrase is just "we follow the standard setup"). Blocked on protocol, not model choice — right model, unverifiable eval.
- **SparseGPT** (unstructured+unlearned, `docs/papers/sparsegpt_frantar2023.pdf`) tests OPT (up to 175B) and BLOOM only — zero occurrences of "LLaMA"/"Llama"/"Mistral" anywhere in the paper (verified via full-text search, not assumption). Predates both model families' release (ICML 2023, before Llama-2/Mistral-7B existed). Blocked on model family, not protocol.
- **LEAP** (Mozaffari et al., arXiv 2605.17289 — "Learnable End-to-End Adaptive Pruning") is the unstructured+learned exemplar this project previously flagged as missing (per-weight Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation, gradient-trained mask, frozen base weights — genuinely the right shape). Tests Qwen-2.5-0.5B, Gemma-3-1B, LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B, LLaMA-3.1-8B — none overlap our model set (Llama-2-7B, Mistral-7B, GPT-2, OPT-125M), AND evaluates ppl at sequence length 4096 vs. our sliding-window 2048/1024 convention. Blocked on both model family and protocol.

Not a wasted search: LEAP fills the "unstructured+learned at LLM scale" gap this project's related-work research had explicitly flagged as thin ([final-paper-direction.md:45](final-paper-direction.md) area) — real citation now exists even without a runnable comparison. LEAP's own reported training cost (22–276 GPU-hours across their model range on 4×H100, 2000 steps/batch=256/seqlen=4096) is useful external context for this project's own compute-cost honesty section (§9) — other trained-mask methods are not cheap either, this isn't a cost unique to our approach. One LEAP number flagged as surprising, not yet verified beyond the WebFetch summary: Qwen-2.5-0.5B's pruned ppl (11.89 @ 50%, 13.16 @ 60%) reads as *below* their own dense baseline (14.17) — same "pruned beats dense" shape as F19/F23, unconfirmed against the primary PDF table directly (only fetched via a summarizing pass, not eyeballed).

**F23 — CNN/DailyMail summarization case study: a second, independent occurrence of the F19/B5 "pruned beats dense" pattern, now on a real downstream task metric (ROUGE), not just in-domain perplexity — strengthens the case for B5's still-unresolved question, does not resolve it.** First real end-to-end run of `train_pruner_llama2_7b_cnndailymail.py` (λ=0.1, seed=0 — target-only CE-delta objective: article context masked from the loss, CE computed only on the gold summary's tokens, teacher-forced, same training mechanism as WikiText-2, deliberately NOT HellaSwag's accuracy-delta surrogate). CAPPED at max_steps=12000 (never converged — `lr_state` stayed `pre_decay` the whole run), final 19.01% FFN neurons pruned. Held-out CNN/DailyMail test set (11,490 examples, full split): ppl 3.750→2.881 (pruned lower/better). 300-example ROUGE-via-generation sample (greedy decoding): rouge1 18.80→26.61, rouge2 7.60→10.05, rougeL 13.31→18.71 — pruned beats dense on every metric, by a wide margin. Same *shape* as F19 (OPT-125M/WikiText-2, later found substantially attributable to a tokenization bug, F21) and matches B5's specialization hypothesis — but a materially different, arguably stronger kind of evidence: ROUGE is a real generation-quality task metric, not in-domain CE, and this run's tokenization already follows the F21-fixed ctx/full-diff convention from the start (no per-line-BOS bug possible here). **Does not resolve B5's load-bearing open question**: no dense-fine-tune control exists on this data, so "pruning improves summarization" and "fine-tuning improves summarization, sparsity along for free" are indistinguishable from this one run. Also flag: the dense baseline itself may simply be weak here — ROUGE-L 13.31 for a non-instruction-tuned base Llama-2-7B completing a bare "Article: ...\n\nSummary:" prompt is low next to typical fine-tuned CNN/DM numbers (30+), so some or all of the "improvement" could be adapting a poorly-prompted dense baseline rather than genuine pruning-driven specialization. Single run (1 λ, 1 seed, non-converged) — no seed variance, no λ sweep, nothing to compare against yet. Cost: ~3.0 GPU-hours on one RunPod H100 (~$9 at $3/hr) — see engineering_decisions.md. Raw results: `experiments/latest/llama2_7b_cnndailymail/lambda_0.1/`.

**F25 — B5's load-bearing open question is ANSWERED, and it goes against pruning: a plain dense LoRA fine-tune matches the pruned model's ROUGE-L exactly and beats it on perplexity. Task adaptation is the active ingredient, not sparsity.** First matched pruned-vs-dense control on CNN/DailyMail (`prune_and_lora_cnndailymail_ddp.py` vs the new `lora_finetune_dense_cnndailymail_ddp.py`, both DDP x8, 1122 optimizer steps, global effective batch 128, 143,616 examples = 50% of the train split per arm, identical LoRA config r=16/alpha=32/mlp_attn/cosine/base_lr=2e-4 scaled to 1.6e-3, seed 0, ROUGE on the same deterministic 3000-example test prefix). The arms differ ONLY in whether physical surgery ran first — verified programmatically by `compare_pruned_vs_dense_lora.py`, which refuses to print a headline unless every matched knob agrees.

  Final ROUGE-L: **pruned+LoRA 22.93% vs dense+LoRA 22.99% — a difference of −0.06pp**, i.e. a tie far inside the sampling noise of a 3000-example ROUGE estimate. Perplexity (full 11,490-example test split): dense+LoRA **2.774** vs pruned+LoRA **2.979** — dense ahead by 0.205, a real gap, not noise. Dense val CE led at every matched checkpoint (step 250/500/750/1000/1122: 1.2139/1.1766/1.1233/1.1013/1.0991 vs pruned's 1.3229/1.2809/1.2146/1.1867/1.1837), and dense at step 500 already beat pruned's *final* value at 1122.

  **What this kills:** the F19/F23 "pruned beats dense" story as a *quality* claim. The dense arm moved R-L 13.78% → 22.99% (+9.21pp) on LoRA alone with no pruning anywhere; the pruned arm's own headline gain (13.78 → 22.27 surgery-only, → 22.93 with LoRA) lands in the same place. Both roads reach ~23% R-L. B5(a) asked "does a plain dense fine-tune on the same domain-matched data match or beat this?" — it matches on the task metric and beats on ppl. So B5 does collapse into "fine-tuning, dressed up" as far as quality is concerned.

  **What survives, and it is not nothing:** the pruned arm reaches statistically identical ROUGE-L with **4,948,201,472 params vs 6,738,415,616** — 26.6% fewer, 41.36% of FFN neurons removed. That is a legitimate *compression-at-iso-quality* result. The correct framing is no longer "pruning improves summarization" (false, per this run) but "task-specific pruning buys ~27% parameter reduction at no ROUGE-L cost, once both models get the same fine-tune." The ppl gap (2.979 vs 2.774) is the honest price paid for that reduction, and should be reported rather than buried.

  **Caveats.** Single seed, single λ (0.3), single operating point — no seed variance, no sparsity sweep of this control. The −0.06pp point estimate has **no confidence interval**: per-example ROUGE dumps were not saved, so it is not formally established as a tie, only as unseparated at this sample size (see the open item in ideas.md). The pruner checkpoint itself never converged (12,000-step cap, `lr_state` stayed `pre_decay`), same caveat as F23. Raw data: `experiments/latest/llama2_7b_cnndailymail_lora_b5/comparison.txt`.

**F26 — the 300-example ROUGE prefix this project has always used is biased low for PRUNED models specifically, by ~3pp, while being accurate for the dense baseline.** Same model, same weights, same greedy decoding, only the eval sample size changed: pruned (λ=0.3, surgery only) scores R-L **19.07% at N=300 and 22.27% at N=3000** (+3.20pp), while dense zero-shot scores **13.85% at N=300 and 13.78% at N=3000** (−0.07pp, flat). Both are prefix slices (`sample_examples` = `examples[:n]`, no RNG), so N=3000 strictly contains the N=300 set — examples 301–3000 score much higher, but *only for the pruned model*. Mechanism (hypothesis, untested): the pruned model has high per-example variance in generation quality while the non-instruction-tuned dense base is uniformly mediocre completing a bare "Article: … Summary:" prompt, so it has little room to vary; the first-300 prefix happens to land where the pruned model underperforms its own mean. Independently corroborated by the perplexity cross-check being *exact* (0.0000 diff on the full test split) in the same run — the eval pipeline is consistent, only the ROUGE sample size moved. **Consequence:** every CNN/DailyMail ROUGE-L number in the existing record (F23's headline, and the λ sweep table's 18.67/18.71/19.07/17.06 pruned column) understates the pruned models by ~3pp against an essentially correct dense column — so the pruned-minus-dense gaps on record are too SMALL, not uniformly shifted. Re-scoring that sweep at N=3000 is cheap (0.25 s/example at world_size=8) and is logged as an open item.

**F7 — Llama-2-7B/WikiText-2 downstream zero-shot accuracy vs. DISP-LLM diverges from F6's perplexity result: near-parity at light pruning, a real and growing deficit at heavy pruning.** Ran lm-evaluation-harness (PIQA/HellaSwag/WinoGrande/ARC-e/ARC-c/BoolQ/OpenBookQA, 0-shot) on all 8 converged Llama-2-7B/WikiText-2 checkpoints (λ=1.4 excluded, F6) plus dense, on a RunPod H100. Restricted to the 5-task subset DISP-LLM's own Table 3 reports (WinoGrande acc, HellaSwag/ARC-e/ARC-c/PIQA acc-norm — matching metric convention) and compared against DISP-LLM's own published LLaMA-2-7B numbers (dense=68.99; only one other real point, 30%-pruned=58.10, no-weight-update/WikiText-structure row — DISP-LLM's downstream table has just 2 operating points for this model, vs. 5 for the ppl table, so interpolation here is much cruder than F6's). Result: LEP sits within noise (±0.5pp) of the interpolated DISP-LLM line through ~9% total-param pruned, then falls increasingly behind — −1.52pp at 19.79%, **−3.04pp at 28.77%** (LEP's most aggressive converged point, same operating-point label as F6's table). A direct, interpolation-free check against DISP-LLM's own *actually-measured* 30% value (58.10) — a slightly more aggressive ratio than LEP's 28.77% comparison point, i.e. charitable to DISP-LLM, not to us — still shows LEP behind by **2.59pp**. This is the opposite shape from F6: there, LEP starts ahead and the gap narrows toward high sparsity; here, LEP starts at parity and the gap *opens up* toward high sparsity. Mechanism not established — a plausible (untested) candidate is that ppl is a smooth, dense metric measured on text distributionally close to the WikiText-2 corpus the pruner itself was trained against, while the downstream suite is discrete and out-of-domain, so a solution that's very good by the training objective's own yardstick need not be equally good by an unrelated one. **Separate flag, not yet resolved**: fresh eval-time gate reconstruction (`load_pruner_and_gates`) reproduces the training run's own logged %-pruned to <0.05pp at 7/8 checkpoints, but at λ=1.0 gives 44.23% vs. the training log's 44.78% (0.55pp gap) despite matching ppl exactly (6.774 both) — the accuracy numbers above use whatever mask the reconstruction actually produced; the table label uses the training-time figure for consistency with F6. Mechanism unconfirmed (eval-mode-vs-training-mode difference, or borderline STE-threshold gates flipping); small enough not to change this finding's conclusion but real. Caveats: DISP-LLM's own suite is 5 tasks; LEP's run additionally covers BoolQ/OpenBookQA (7-task avg reported separately in `experiments/latest/llama2_7b_downstream/summary.txt`, not used in this comparison since DISP-LLM doesn't report those tasks); single seed, as with every 7B-scale result in this project (F6). Raw data: `experiments/latest/llama2_7b_downstream/summary.json`.
