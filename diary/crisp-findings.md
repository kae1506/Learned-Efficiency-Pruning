## Findings

**F1 — Weights alone encode redundancy.** A BiLSTM reading only weight matrices prunes \~60–79% of an MNIST MLP at\<0.1–3.7pp drop. No data, no gradients needed.

**F2 — pg19's zero-shot CE screening numbers (50K-token sample) were unrepresentative; the real gap survives at proper sample size.** `zero_shot_ce_check.py`'s original 50K-token check gave GPT-2 CE=3.326 (ppl 27.83), OPT-125M CE=2.910 (ppl 18.35). Re-run at 245K tokens (matching the sweep scripts' own `n_test_tokens` default): GPT-2 CE=3.9255 (ppl 50.68), OPT-125M CE=3.4508 (ppl 31.53) — both roughly doubled, confirming pg19's book-to-book heterogeneity makes a small deterministic-streamed sample unrepresentative, not a GPT-2-specific bug. The B6-relevant part survives: gap = GPT-2_CE − OPT_CE was +0.416 nats at 50K, +0.4746 nats at 245K — same direction, similar (slightly larger) magnitude. Treat 27.83/18.35 as superseded; 50.68/31.53 are the correct zero-shot pg19 baselines to compare sweep results against.

**F3 — Pruner-capacity ablation (B7b) doesn't cleanly resolve whether high-λ underperformance is a capacity bottleneck or an inherently harder landscape — it surfaces a third, unanticipated confound: convergence-window validity is itself capacity-dependent.** `train_pruner_pg19_capacity_scaling.py` (embed_dim=128, lstm_hidden=256 — 2.257× base params; the script's own default of 144/216 was overridden for this run) vs. base-size pruner, matched at 4 λ (0.05, 0.3, 0.8, 1.6) × 2 models. Mixed result: 3/8 points improved (gpt2 λ=0.8: ppl −5.52%, fewer steps; opt125m λ=0.05: −1.19%; opt125m λ=1.6: −3.43%), 3/8 got worse (gpt2 λ=0.05: +1.80%; gpt2 λ=1.6: +1.36%; opt125m λ=0.3: **+6.22%**), 2/8 flat. No clean split by "flagged high-λ trouble point" — GPT-2 and OPT-125M disagree on which of 0.8/1.6 benefits from more capacity. The concerning case: opt125m λ=0.3 (a "should stay stable" control point) converged in *fewer* steps (900→600) at *worse* ppl, and its gap-diagnostic trajectory shows why — pct_pruned climbed 28.6%→32.2%→35.5% in a steady monotonic trend, not noise, yet still satisfied the block-mean flatness check (5%/1pp tolerance, 250-step window) and got called converged mid-climb. Mechanism: a bigger/more expressive pruner can move through weight space faster per step, so a window size tuned against the base pruner's dynamics can mistake a genuinely-still-moving trend for a plateau simply because more of the trajectory completes before the window closes. Window size and pruner capacity were both held "fixed" as the wrong assumption — window validity isn't capacity-invariant. Next step (not yet run): rerun the 2x-capacity ablation with a longer/stricter window to see if that closes the opt125m λ=0.3 gap, before drawing further conclusions about H1.

**F4 — A third capacity point (half-size pruner, tmp5) shows NO meaningful, monotonic effect of pruner capacity on %pruned, ppl quality, or convergence speed — F3's apparent "2x helps at some λ" pattern looks like noise now that there's a third point to check it against.** Three-way comparison (half=0.4678x, base=1x, 2.26x — see F3 — all at the same 4λ × 2 models = 8 matched points, 1 seed each): 0/8 points show a monotonic half<base<2.26x ordering on %pruned (avg spread 8.6% of base, non-directional); 0/8 monotonic on ppl rise (avg spread only 4.2% of base — the cleanest null of the three); steps-to-converge is the noisiest metric by far (avg spread 32.8%) but *still* only 3/8 monotonic, i.e. the metric with the most absolute variation shows the least systematic relationship to capacity. A genuine capacity effect should move the same direction from half→base→2.26x; it doesn't, anywhere, on any of the three axes. Reframes H1/B7b: within this ~5x capacity range, pruner size is not the binding constraint on outcome — effort is better spent on F3's window-validity mechanism (a fixed 250-step convergence window may not mean the same thing at different pruner sizes / different λ difficulty) than on further capacity scaling. Caveat: single seed per config throughout (as with F3) — cannot cleanly separate "truly no effect" from "a small real effect below single-seed noise," but the noise floor itself (8.6%/4.2%) is now at least characterized.

**F5 — Layer-budget vs. within-layer-selection decomposition: WITHIN-layer neuron selection is doing critical, irreplaceable work — F4's capacity-insensitivity is NOT evidence the pruner "isn't doing anything."** Motivated directly by F4's suspicious-looking null result. Architecturally the pruner's two jobs are separable (BiLSTM's `context_bias` is one scalar per layer, cannot discriminate within a layer — 100% of within-layer selection is the row-encoder's job). Test: take a trained pruner checkpoint's *exact* per-layer budget (`per_layer_kept`, the BiLSTM's discovered counts) but replace WITHIN-layer neuron choice with simple activation-magnitude ranking (same criterion as F22's baseline — mean post-ReLU activation, keep highest/prune lowest — NOT weight magnitude), same per-layer count as the real mask. Checkpoints: `tmp3/pg19_converge_sweep/{gpt2,opt125m}/lambda_0.3/pruner.pt` (base-size pruner, mid-range λ). Result: catastrophic degradation for both models — gpt2: real pruned ppl 40.698 → decomposed 87.706 (**+47.0**, worse than not pruning at all, orig=50.683); opt125m: real 35.538 → decomposed 48.707 (**+13.2**, also worse than orig=31.528). Handing the heuristic the *correct* per-layer budget (an advantage F22's own baseline never had) still fails badly — bigger gap than F22 found even. Conclusion: within-layer selection is real, substantial, necessary work a magnitude-style heuristic cannot approximate, even with perfect budget information. F4's capacity result is better read as "this particular scoring function, whatever it is, happens to be representable by a very small network (down to embed_dim=32)" — not "the row-encoder isn't computing anything meaningful." Design notes in engineering_decisions.md.

**F6 — Llama-2-7B/WikiText-2, matched against DISP-LLM's own published numbers on the identical model+dataset, beats DISP-LLM at every converged operating point up to ~29% total-param pruned — and the one point past that which loses is independently diagnosable as broken, not a real data point.** 9-λ convergence-based sweep (`train_pruner_llama2_7b_wikitext2.py`, dense ppl=4.903), converted to total-param-pruned % via Llama-2-7B's 64.24% MLP parameter share, compared against DISP-LLM Table 1's LLaMA-2-7B/no-weight-update column (dense=5.12; 20%→6.10, 30%→6.85, 40%→8.11, 50%→9.84, linearly interpolated to our exact %s). Result: **8 of 8 converged points beat the interpolated DISP-LLM curve** — by −0.45 ppl at the low end (5.4-6.6% total pruned), narrowing steadily to a near-tie at 28.77% (+0.016 ppl, essentially noise). The 9th point (λ=1.4, 32.58% total) loses by +0.494 ppl, but **never converged** — `lr_state` stayed `pre_decay` for the full 18,000-step cap, meaning `check_converged` never returned `True` even once (not a false-trigger-then-fail like B9 was built to catch — a total absence of any trigger). Diagnosed the failure directly from `gap_diagnostic.csv`: `pct_pruned` in the last 20 checkpoints (steps 14200-18000) still swings an 8.2pp band (48.5-56.7%) with essentially the same noise level as the *first* 20 checkpoints (mean 51.4%→52.8%, stdev 2.81→1.74 — barely tightening after 14,000 more steps), and `max_layer_delta_pct` averages ~30pp *even in the final checkpoints* — individual layers are still swinging by up to 47.8pp between 200-step checkpoints at step 18,000. This is a genuinely oscillating equilibrium, not a slow monotonic drift more steps would resolve. Mechanism (not yet confirmed, best current hypothesis): at λ=1.4 the sparsity term (λ·mean(gate) ≈ 0.66-0.8) is now comparable in magnitude to the CE-cost term (≈0.37-0.46), and the LR is held at a constant `1e-3` regardless of λ (never scaled down for high-λ operating points, since the B9 decay mechanism never triggers to bring it down) — a plausible recipe for sustained oscillation around a ridge in the loss landscape rather than convergence to a fixed point. **Directly re-tested the "increase pruner capacity" fix against F3/F4's own prior evidence and it doesn't hold**: F3/F4 already found no meaningful monotonic capacity effect on %pruned/ppl/convergence-speed across an 8-point matched grid (half/base/2.26x) — capacity controls representational expressiveness of the row-encoder/BiLSTM, not the STE-threshold oscillation dynamics under a fixed-LR gradient signal, so there's no mechanistic reason to expect it to help here either. The indicated fix is LR/λ-coupling (lower or λ-scaled initial LR for high-sparsity operating points), not architecture size — an optimization-dynamics problem, not a capacity problem. Caveat: F3/F4 were run at GPT-2/OPT-125M/pg19 scale, not 7B — applying that finding here is a reasonable prior, not a proven transfer. Single seed throughout (as with every convergence-based sweep in this project) — no seed-variance estimate at this operating point. Full sweep, DISP-LLM comparison table, and mechanism note in `past-work.md`/`final-paper-direction.md`.
